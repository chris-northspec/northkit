# Compliance sources (S0-2)

Official sources only (HSE, legislation.gov.uk, the IET's public pages). Summaries are our own words; nothing is copied. Checked **17 Nov 2026**. Some pages were read through a summarising tool, so **re-read the page before quoting it in product copy**. Items we could not verify are marked **Unverified**.

NorthKit stores records and reminds you when they are due. The customer's competent person decides intervals and results (ADR 0003).

## LOLER 1998 (lifting equipment)
| Source | What it says, for a kit owner |
| --- | --- |
| [HSE: thorough examinations and inspections of lifting equipment](https://www.hse.gov.uk/work-equipment-machinery/thorough-examinations-lifting-equipment.htm) | A **competent person** has the knowledge, experience and independence to find defects and judge them; they should not be the person who routinely maintains the kit. Intervals: **6 months** for equipment that lifts people and for lifting accessories, **12 months** for other lifting equipment, unless a **written examination scheme** drawn up by a competent person says otherwise. The report states the examination date, the next due date and any defect that is or could become dangerous. It goes to the user; serious defects are also reported to the enforcing authority by the examiner. Records go with equipment that is hired out or sold. Between thorough examinations, in-service **inspections** catch deterioration; their frequency comes from risk assessment and the maker's advice. |
| [Regulation 9: thorough examination and inspection](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1998/2307/regulation/9) | Examination before first use (with a narrow exception for new kit with a recent declaration of conformity), after installation where safety depends on it, then at the intervals above, and after exceptional circumstances. Kit moving between undertakings must carry physical evidence of its last examination. |
| [Regulation 10: reports and defects](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1998/2307/regulation/10) | The examiner tells the employer at once about a dangerous defect and sends a written report as soon as practicable. The employer must not use the equipment until the defect is put right. |
| [HSE index page for LOLER](https://www.hse.gov.uk/work-equipment-machinery/loler.htm) | Links to the subtopics. |

**Unverified:** how long reports must be kept (the pages we read give no period; HSE points to its guidance INDG422, which we did not open). Do not state a retention period for LOLER reports.

## PUWER 1998 (work equipment)
| Source | What it says |
| --- | --- |
| [HSE: work equipment inspection duties](https://www.hse.gov.uk/work-equipment-machinery/inspection.htm) | Inspection is needed after installation or moving where safety depends on it, at suitable intervals where deterioration could cause danger, and after exceptional events. **Keep results at least until the next inspection**; records need not be on paper and electronic ones must be secure and available to enforcers. Don't use equipment that needs inspection unless you know it was inspected; hired kit should carry evidence of its last inspection (a report, tag or label). Frequencies come from risk assessment, the maker's advice and experience. **Pre-use checks** are quick user checks, normally not recorded. |
| [HSE: maintaining work equipment](https://www.hse.gov.uk/work-equipment-machinery/maintenance.htm) | Keep equipment in good working order, keep any maintenance log up to date, use competent people, follow the maker's instructions. |
| [HSE: L22 Safe use of work equipment](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/books/l22.htm) | The approved code of practice and guidance (4th edition, 2014). |

**Unverified:** the regulation numbers on the maintenance page, and the regulation text itself (not opened).

## Portable appliance testing (PAT)
| Source | What it says |
| --- | --- |
| [HSE: portable appliance testing FAQ](https://www.hse.gov.uk/electricity/faq-portable-appliance-testing.htm) | The law requires electrical equipment to be maintained to prevent danger, but **does not require annual PAT**, or say who must do it or how often. Frequency depends on the equipment and where it is used (HSE's own examples range from months to years). User checks help. A formal visual inspection may need a competent person; a combined inspection and test needs more skill and suitable test equipment. Records and labels aren't a legal duty but show a scheme exists. Hire suppliers are strongly advised to inspect and test before each hire. |
| [IET Code of Practice for In-service Inspection and Testing of Electrical Equipment, 5th edition](https://shop.theiet.org/code-of-practice-for-in-service-inspection-and-testing-of-electrical-equipment-5th-edition) | A paid publication (2020). The 5th edition is the latest we found; **Unverified** whether a newer edition exists, so recheck before saying "current edition". The IET offers free model record forms, which we may cite but not copy. |

**Unverified:** the Code's risk-based interval tables (paywalled). NorthKit never reproduces them: the customer sets an interval.

## Work at height, ladders, harnesses
| Source | What it says |
| --- | --- |
| [Work at Height Regulations 2005, regulation 12](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2005/735/regulation/12) | Inspect equipment after installation, at suitable intervals and after exceptional events; **record results and keep them until the next inspection**; kit moving between undertakings carries evidence of its last inspection. A LOLER examination counts, with its report as the record. |
| [HSE: inspecting the condition of ladders](https://www.hse.gov.uk/work-at-height/ladders/inspecting-condition-of-ladders.htm) | A detailed visual inspection by a competent person at fixed intervals (**no period is set**), recorded; follow the maker's instructions. Pre-use checks by the user each working day are not recorded; problems go to a manager. |
| [HSE: work at height FAQ](https://www.hse.gov.uk/work-at-height/faqs.htm) | Equipment must be properly inspected and maintained; it gives no harness or ladder interval. |
| [HSE INDG367: inspecting fall-arrest equipment of webbing or rope](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/indg367.htm) | A leaflet on inspection regimes for harnesses and similar kit (PDF not read). |

**Unverified:** HSE gives **no** "6 to 12 months" figure for harnesses in anything we read. Treat that as the makers' and industry practice, labelled so. We also didn't open the PPE at Work Regulations 1992.

**HSE sets no calibration interval** in what we found. Annual calibration, the maker's recommendation and UKAS or ISO 17025 traceability are **industry practice, not HSE rules**. NorthKit's default is an editable "confirm with a competent person" value.

## Pre-use and user checks
HSE separates three things: a **pre-use check** (quick, by the user, usually unrecorded), a **detailed visual inspection** (competent person, recorded) and a **thorough examination** (LOLER, competent person, report). NorthKit records all three but never mixes them up: a ticked pre-use check is not an inspection.

## What the product must not claim
- That NorthKit "certifies", "approves", "passes" or "makes you compliant". The duties rest on the employer; examinations are done by a competent person.
- That it performs or replaces a thorough examination, an inspection, PAT, calibration or a competent person.
- That HSE sets annual PAT, a harness or ladder interval, a calibration interval or a LOLER report retention period.
- That 6 and 12 months are "the legal interval" without the written-scheme alternative, or for kit LOLER does not cover.
- That a stored scan is a "statutory report" or "legal record", or satisfies the duty to keep evidence for enforcers.
- That a ticked pre-use check is a formal inspection.
- That it reports defects to HSE or an enforcing authority: that duty lies with the examiner and the employer.
- That it is "IET-aligned" or reproduce the Code's tables.

Say instead: **"NorthKit stores your records and reminds you when they are due. You remain responsible for having examinations done by a competent person."**
