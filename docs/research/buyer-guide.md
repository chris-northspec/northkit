# S0-3: Buyer guide and capture sheet (three UK conversations)

For Gate 1 (optional: Chris may lift it, as for ClearRate). One page to print and take into each call. Ask about last month, not about our idea; show the idea only at the end; the only thing that counts is a commitment.

**Who:** UK businesses that own and issue kit, in four groups: **contractors with 10 to 200 staff** (building, electrical, mechanical), **plant and tool hire depots**, **facilities contractors**, and **manufacturers' maintenance teams**. Whoever looks after the kit: a contracts manager, depot manager, health and safety manager, workshop or stores lead, or the owner. **Not** Chris's employer, its customers or its competitors' staff. Public business contact details only. Chris's own time and kit.

**Outreach (email or LinkedIn, no pitch):**
> Subject: how you keep track of tools and equipment
>
> Hi [name], I'm looking into how firms keep track of their tools and equipment: where it is, who has it, and when it's due an inspection or test. I'm not selling anything yet; I want to understand how it's really done before I build anything. Could I borrow 20 minutes? Thanks, Chris

## Questions, in order (20 to 30 minutes)

**1. What they own and where it is**
1. Roughly how many items of tools and equipment do you own, and of what kinds (power tools, ladders, lifting gear, harnesses, test meters, plant)? How many are hired in?
2. Where does it live: depots, vans, yards, job sites? How many of each?
3. How do you know where an item is today? Walk me through the last time someone needed a specific item.

**2. Who has it**
4. How does kit get issued and returned? Who writes it down, and where (a book, a spreadsheet, tags, an app)?
5. Last time something went missing: what was it, how did you find out, what did it cost?
6. How often do you do a stocktake, and how long does it take?

**3. Safe and in date**
7. Which inspections, examinations and tests do you have to keep up with (thorough examinations of lifting gear, PUWER inspections, PAT, calibration, ladder and harness checks)? How is each one tracked?
8. Who does them: your own people, or an outside inspector? How do the reports and certificates come back, and where are they kept?
9. Last time something was used out of date, or you found an overdue item: what happened?
10. What happens when kit fails a check? How do you make sure nobody takes it?
11. Has an auditor, insurer, client or the HSE asked to see your records? What did you have to dig out, and how long did it take?

**4. What they've tried**
12. What do you use now (spreadsheets, paper tags, a hire system, a tool tracker, an inspection firm's portal)? What do you like and dislike?
13. Have you looked at a tool, asset or inspection product? Which, and why didn't you buy it?

**5. Hosted or self-hosted**
14. Would you rather this ran on your own server or as a hosted service? Who looks after your servers now?
15. Any client or security rules about where data may be kept?

**6. Only now, the idea (two minutes, then stop talking)**
> "A register that runs on your own server. Every item gets a QR label. You check kit out to a person, van or site in two taps, even without signal. It schedules inspections and keeps the examiner's report against the item. Kit that fails a check is quarantined so it can't be issued. It records what your competent person decides; it doesn't certify anything."

16. What would it need to do on day one for you to use it?
17. What would stop you buying it?

**7. Price and commitment (the only answers that count)**
18. What would it be worth to you a year? (Let them name a number first.)
19. If it did what you need, would you pay **£[test price]**? Test one figure per call, taken from the opportunity report tiers, and say whether it is a yearly price or one-off.
20. Would you put that in writing: a paid pilot, a deposit or a letter of intent before we build the inspections module (Sprint 4)?
